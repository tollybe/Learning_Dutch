# Learning Dutch — sustainable, transferable practice

Updated: 2026-11-09 · Proposed in [PR #1](https://github.com/tollybe/Learning_Dutch/pull/1).

## North star and success criteria

Grow from a practical B1 foundation toward natural B2 Dutch, with optional C1 analysis and deliberately *onnodig bekakt* style. ChatGPT is the tutor, daily life is the practice context, and GitHub is the low-maintenance project record.

Optimise in this order:
1. **Sustainability:** the learner still enjoys the single-word reminder; there is no study debt.
2. **Transfer:** the learner can use familiar language independently in a different conversation or situation.
3. **Accuracy and range:** communication becomes clearer, more idiomatic and better adapted to context.

**Never optimise for streaks, long answers, ever-increasing word counts, or elaborate dashboards.** The learner works a busy 40-hour week. The system, not the learner, should carry the administrative load.

## Fixed commitments (do not add any)

| Existing activity | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| 08:00 daily vocabulary reminder | Exactly one useful word **or established expression** with a natural example and one optional ~30-second task. On some days, reuse an older item in a new context instead of insisting on novelty. |
| Wednesday and Saturday short B1 practice | One realistic dialogue and one optional task; replace rather than stack the daily word's active challenge when they overlap. |
| Sunday vocabulary archive | Continue the existing archive, with no extra learner work; verify GitHub updates before claiming the integration is reliable. |
| Conversations with Daan, his parents and friends | Authentic, voluntary interactions. No planned quizzes or obligations for other people. |
| Rosie walks | Optional audio-only listening and spoken responses *within* an existing walk; never read, type or look at the phone while moving. |

**No catch-up queue.** Missing a day produces no overdue items or negative judgements. Reading only, listening only, skipping or asking for an explanation are all acceptable.

## Modes — choose one rather than stack activities

| Mode | When | Examples | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum | A demanding day | Read or hear the word and its sentence; optionally skip | 0–1 min |
| Everyday (default) | Normal day | Say or type one sentence using today's word | 2–3 min |
| Rosie walk | Already outdoors | Hands-free: repeat, answer a single question, or describe the route or surroundings | 1–5 min inside the walk |
| Curious | Have time and interest | Short dialogue, Dutch media, article analysis, posh-register experiment | 5–15+ min, optional |

Rosie-walk safety: give traffic and Rosie full attention, avoid screen interaction, and abandon the exercise whenever listening or speaking would distract from the environment. Voice features may be useful but are **not** a validated pronunciation exam.

## Balanced skill development

An expert concern is that vocabulary + written ChatGPT answers alone do not demonstrate conversational Dutch. Keep the micro-format but vary the *type* of practice over time.

| Skill | Light-touch opportunity | Evidence that would actually matter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading/recognition | Understand the example, sign, message or short article | Comprehension in another context, not simply seeing it |
| Listening | Hear a short, natural phrase during a Rosie walk or in conversation | Understand a new utterance without a written transcript |
| Production | Respond with one original written **or spoken** sentence | Use the target structure appropriately without being supplied it |
| Interaction | Ask and answer a follow-up with Daan or ChatGPT Voice | Sustain meaning and respond to an unexpected turn |
| Pragmatics/register | Compare everyday wording with formal or playful aristocratic Dutch | Choose an appropriate expression for the audience |

Do **not** force every skill into every day. This table is for the tutor's longer-term balance, not a learner checklist.

## Retrieval and spaced reuse without backlog

- Daily novelty is motivating, but the tutor should sometimes select a previously encountered word, collocation or structure for **retrieval in a fresh situation**.
- Prefer an initial question that lets the learner recall before revealing the model answer; provide the answer promptly if asked or when the learner is tired.
- A correct answer with the target word visible is **prompted use**, not spontaneous mastery. An answer in an unexpected context provides stronger, but still limited, evidence.
- Use small, opportunistic reviews rather than fixed numbers of repetitions or a failing-streak system.
- Schedule no extra reminders for review.

## Default adaptive ChatGPT feedback

For an answer that the learner actually provides:
1. Acknowledge what successfully communicates the intended meaning.
2. Correct **at most the most useful issue**, in one or two sentences. If meaning is substantially obscured, briefly ask for clarification rather than pretending a single correction can fix everything.
3. When it feels helpful, invite **one** tiny self-correction before giving the full answer. Do not insist on retries.
4. Classify the issue accurately: grammar, vocabulary/collocation, clarity, naturalness, register, or an acceptable alternative. Do not mark a stylistic preference or a Dutch regional variant as a mistake.
5. On future occasions, vary the setting and gently revisit **demonstrated** recurring weaknesses. Do not infer a deficit from missed or unanswered reminders.
6. If the response is good, say so. Never invent an error for the sake of feedback.

Correction is **opt-out** at any time: “let's just chat in Dutch” means the interaction can proceed without continuous analysis. Avoid interrupting ordinary conversations with corrections. If uncertain, admit uncertainty; speakers' preferences are not universal laws.

## Use of the social environment

- Daan can optionally answer *“Hoe zou jij dit zeggen?”* when a question arises naturally. He is a partner, **not a standing tutor**.
- Daan's family and Dutch-speaking friends are authentic conversation partners, not assessment tools.
- Dutch books, signage, news, podcasts and other resources can supply useful contexts. Deep B2–C1 linguistic decomposition remains opt-in.
- Keep ChatGPT as the default interface; GitHub should not be opened just to complete a lesson.

## Low-stakes evaluation, not surveillance

Every few weeks, **if invited**, offer a one-minute unprompted explanation or conversation based on a familiar real-life scenario. Avoid specifying the target word in advance. Look at comprehensibility, ability to respond, vocabulary range and register—not the number of advanced clauses. A single check is not proof of mastery.

At an optional monthly reflection ask only: “Still enjoyable?”, “Where was Dutch useful in real life?”, and “What is too much?” If learning becomes burdensome, remove features before adding any.

The distinction between encountered, recognised, prompted, independent and delayed use is described in [QUALITY_AND_EVIDENCE.md](QUALITY_AND_EVIDENCE.md). Do not turn it into a numeric learner score without a validated assessment method.

## Privacy and implementation boundaries

The repository is public as of the initial review. Do not automatically upload private answers, audio, partner/family conversations, personally identifying situations or voice transcripts. Only explicitly consented, minimal, preferably abstract learning evidence may later be recorded. No automated feedback-to-GitHub synchronisation exists yet; the current system documents the workflow and creates project-status reports.
